THIS IS THE PART THAT ISN'T IN THE BROCHURE

On their own, plate reads look harmless. Linked together, they become a pattern of life — where you sleep, when you leave, who you're with, what changed this week, and where you'll probably go next. An algorithm flagging a break in your routine as an "anomaly" isn't public safety. It's profiling, and the network running in NC can build this on any resident it has seen.

POINT THE LENS BACK

The officials above approved this system for everyone else. In Resident Mode, the subject becomes you — same cameras, same hotlists, the same 90-day retention, the same searches that need no warrant. The only thing that changes is the name on the plate, and a profile like this is already waiting to be pulled for anyone who drives in North Carolina.

You just watched a system build someone's life-pattern in seconds. A real one is running across North Carolina right now.

What this is

A DEFLOCK NC CIVIC-EDUCATION DEMONSTRATION

OVERWATCH is a parody. It borrows the look and the powers of a real automated-license-plate-reader dashboard and points them the other way — at the officials who approve North Carolina's surveillance programs, the staff who run them, and the agencies that search the data. Flip to Resident Mode and it turns those same powers on ordinary people going about ordinary days.

The point is simple: it's hard to feel what a surveillance system does when you're not the one inside it. This turns the lens around so anyone can see it from the watched side.

  • Every plate, location, timestamp, association and prediction here is invented.
  • Plates use a synthetic OVR- format — not real North Carolina plates, and no real vehicle is tracked.
  • No home addresses appear. Only public roles and public buildings, and only as commentary.
  • The Sharing panel is the exception — those figures are real, from public-records research.

The ask was never about one vendor. It's about consent: surveillance this powerful should be decided by a public vote and written into a binding ordinance — not left to a setting any police chief can change.
